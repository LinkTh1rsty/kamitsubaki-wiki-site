## Home and Database

Search for a person, song, album or keyword directly from Home; “Search full text” opens site search. Home shows collection counts and six people to start with. Database keeps the full classification and advanced filters. Site updates link to published announcements; the archive's preface can be expanded when you want to read it, and its existing direct links still work.

Database offers catalogue, name and year sorting. Undated records stay last in both year directions. Applied conditions can be removed individually. Language switching keeps the query and layout; browser Back and Forward restore collection, filter, sorting and layout changes. Clearing filters keeps sorting and layout.

## Music works

The home page previews tracks and releases; the [music directory](/en/database/music/) contains the full catalog. Open cover artwork for a larger view. Tracks and releases are separate records connected by structured data; check the relevant record for dates, performers, and credits.

## Articles

[Articles](/en/articles/) lists reviewed, public articles in the current content language. Search, filter by category, and load later results as needed. The article reader includes an outline. Links to encyclopedia entries are explicit relationships selected during submission; a link or name in the body alone is insufficient.

## Explore the archive

The [chronicle](/en/chronicle/) groups dated events into eras, with graphic and list views sharing search and year filters. The [reference gallery](/en/gallery/) shows approved images by character and set; one set may contain several images. The [relationship network](/en/explore/relations/) follows structured links, and the [world guide](/en/explore/world/) offers themed routes. Experimental games and chat remain under [LABs](/en/labs/), away from the reference catalog.

The gallery begins with a character directory and recent sets. Within a character, filter by form; a set without one appears under **Unclassified**. Each set occupies one cover card. Open it to browse published images in order. Pending, returned, and approved-but-not-yet-synchronized images do not count as public.

## Find a connection between two entries

In the [relationship network](/en/explore/relations/), enter the full names of two endpoints or select suggestions with entry IDs, then choose **Find a connection**. The shortest recorded route shows each link's type, original direction and evidence. Swap endpoints or explore from any entry along the path. **Include chronicle events** allows connections through shared events. Distance describes the current archive's links, not relationship strength. Share the current URL; browser Back and Forward restore submitted queries.

## Graph and path comparison

[Graph](/en/graph/) is available from the main navigation, Explore and each entry’s connection link. The whole graph retains every archive node, including isolated entries. Enable chronicle events to add explicitly referenced events. Filter node and relation types, or explore one to three steps around a node. Search locates nodes without cutting the graph; the directory can select nodes outside the neighborhood.

Drag empty space to pan or a node to reposition it. Use the buttons, Ctrl + wheel or a touch pinch to zoom; **Fit graph** restores the overview. A focused canvas supports arrows, plus/minus and Home. Use the directory to select nodes with a keyboard. Node details retain the recorded direction and evidence, link to the entry and neighbors, and can set either path endpoint. Save a graph image or share the URL; Back and Forward restore filters.

**Compare connection paths** shows up to three shorter routes without repeated nodes. Different recorded relations or credit roles can distinguish routes with the same nodes. Queries use the current node, relation and chronicle filters. **Show on graph** highlights and fits a route, returning to the whole graph from a neighborhood view. This does not enumerate every possible path or measure relationship strength. The older connections page remains available.

## Live archive and song history

The [live archive](/en/explore/lives/) combines performer, year, event type, venue type and text filters. Performers include explicitly listed headliners and guests; group membership does not imply participation. Date-range records can be found in every year they cover. Open the original entry or add a missing setlist. Filters are shareable and restored by browser history.

Live entries include documented facts and setlists. A song's performance history uses only explicit song IDs in setlists, retaining multiple renditions in one event. Title-only tracks are not guessed matches. Missing records do not prove a song was never performed, and the earliest record does not establish its debut. Exhibitions and general events without songs do not receive missing-setlist prompts.

## Creators and collaborations

[Creators and collaborations](/en/explore/credits/) combines creator, performer, role and release-year filters. Creator and role must match the same credit row. Selecting a credit shows an overview of all its documented songs and collaborating performers; filters narrow the work list below. Click a collaborator or year to continue exploring.

Name-only credits remain separate from similarly named entities. Several roles on one song count as one credited work. Statistics cover only songs with recorded credits and do not imply that unrecorded collaborations never happened.

## Discovery interests and daily routes

[Discovery](/en/explore/discovery/) recommends entries using up to three artists or creators, entry type, recorded style or tags, keywords and a year range. Choose one year or a two- or five-year window; undated records do not match a known year. **Explore** applies the preferences retained in the URL. Moving between route stops preserves unapplied edits. Style coverage is currently limited; metadata keywords can find authored descriptions or credits, and unmatched conditions can be broadened explicitly.

Each recommendation explains a direct or two-step connection, recorded tag, year or keyword match. Expand its evidence to check direction and sources. Multiple interests match any selected entity’s explicit connections, without inferring participation from group membership. Recommendations balance entry types and use no private account data or browsing history.

Daily routes update on UTC dates and support three, five or seven stops and variations. The same archive, date and preferences reproduce a route across five languages. Preferences choose the starting thread; later stops follow recorded connections across types and years. Nodes do not repeat, and shorter routes show their actual endpoint. Follow previous or next stops, or restart from any entry. Sharing fixes the current route date.

Articles use public list metadata and explicit entity references. Articles without references can be discovered independently; titles do not become invented relationships. If the public index is unavailable, other entries remain usable and coverage is shown. Without scripts, the page displays the dated build-time route and initial recommendations.

## Missing material

Try an alias, original-language name, or parent category. A newly submitted entry, article, or image does not appear publicly until its own review and publication steps are complete. Contributors can inspect the real status in [Creator Center](/en/account/creator/).

## Different ways to explore one subject

For a creator's participation, start with the entry and related records. For what happened in a year, use the chronicle. For images of a form, use the gallery. For entity connections, use the relation network. Each indexes different structured data; adding prose to one entry does not automatically update every view.

The chronicle's graphic view shows era spans; the list is better for checking individual dates and sources. Both share current filters. Do not invent dates merely to populate a timeline. The gallery displays **set → images** and can be narrowed by character. A submitted set may have only some images approved, so public image count may differ from batch count.

The article list contains public writing in the current content language. Search, category, and pagination help locate it, and the URL retains filter state. For reference facts, return to an entry and its sources: an article's argument does not automatically alter encyclopedia properties.

## Story Mode guided routes

[Story Mode](/en/explore/stories/) offers seven introductions: KAF, V.W.P, Kamitsubaki City, KAF to Kaika, PIEDPIPER, Kanzaki Iori and reading a stage. Each has six chapters with questions, source-backed records and optional existing media. Suggested guide lengths exclude the full duration of songs, videos and original works.

Moving between chapters does not mark them read. Use “Mark read and continue” to save completion in this device’s browser. Languages share progress; a shared link contains only the chapter position. Restart clears only the current route. After reading all chapters, continue with another route, Discovery or Graph.

Players load only on request and close when you change chapters. Original links remain available. Story summaries can be expanded deliberately, and editorial reading order does not imply a shared plot chronology. All chapters remain readable without scripts or persistent storage.


## Fan Archive tools

The [Explore portal](/en/explore/) groups discovery, records, time and places, community, research and sources. [Music Universe](/en/explore/music/) connects versions, credits, media and recorded performances; [Atlas](/en/explore/atlas/) lets you arrange real-place routes; [Visual Evolution](/en/explore/visual/) uses recorded visual dates.

[My Observatory](/en/explore/observatory/) offers voluntary listening, visit, ownership and reading records. Records start private and can be exported. Published passports still display only individually public records. [Collections](/en/explore/collections/) support drafts, follow and fork; exhibitions need independent review. [Challenges](/en/explore/challenges/) come from actual missing fields and translations. Account synchronization and shared actions require a connected service.

[Ask](/en/explore/ask/) recognizes a limited query grammar: inspect conditions before execution. [Compare](/en/explore/compare/), [Evidence](/en/explore/evidence/) and [Languages](/en/explore/languages/) help check records. Statistics describe the current sample. [Radio](/en/explore/radio/) uses a separate playback window for continuous playback across pages. [Physical Archive](/en/explore/physical/) matches only recorded barcodes and asks you to confirm the edition. [This day](/en/explore/today/) matches only exact recorded dates.
